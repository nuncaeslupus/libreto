The back room was smaller than she had pictured. A table, two chairs, a calendar from a brewery that had closed before the war. On the table a single glass, and a ring of damp beside it where another glass had stood.

“Kit?” she said, to nobody.

Nobody answered. Somewhere above her a tap was running. She stood very still and listened to it until it stopped, and then she went back up the stairs, slowly this time, and out through the bar without looking at anyone.

Lena did not look at her either.

Lena’s Warning

The Blue Lamp was half empty by ten, which was how Vega liked it. The jukebox had given up on the evening, and the only sound was Lena drying glasses that were already dry, one after another, as if the cloth could rub the night off them.

“You’re early,” Lena said without looking up. “Or late. I can never tell with you.”

Vega took the stool at the end of the bar, the one with a view of the back-room door. “Has Kit been in?”

“Kit who?” Lena set a glass down hard enough to make it ring. “Half the city’s called Kit.”
