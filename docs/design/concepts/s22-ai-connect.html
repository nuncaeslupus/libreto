CHAPTER 7

Lena’s warning

The Blue Lamp was half empty by ten, which was how Vega liked it. The jukebox had given up on the evening, and the only sound was Lena drying glasses that were already dry, one after another, as if the cloth could rub the night off them.

“You’re early,” Lena said without looking up. “Or late. I can never tell with you.”

Vega took the stool at the end of the bar, the one with a view of the back-room door. “Has Kit been in?”

“Kit who?” Lena set a glass down hard enough to make it ring. “Half the city’s called Kit.”

“Mine. Thin, nervous, writes everything down in a little green ledger.” Vega slid a coin across the counter and left her hand on it. “He said he’d meet me here at nine.”

Lena looked at the coin, then at the door behind her, then at nothing at all. “Nobody came in at nine. Nobody’s been in that room since Tuesday.”

It was a good lie. Vega had heard worse from people who were paid to tell them. But there was a wet ring on the bar by the back-room door, and a second glass upside down on the drainer, still beaded.

“Lena,” she said quietly, “if he’s in trouble, I’d rather hear it from you.”

“Then hear this.” Lena leaned close enough that Vega could smell the lemon on her hands. “Go home. Don’t come back to the Blue Lamp tomorrow. And don’t ask Kit what he saw.”
