Lena looked at the coin, then at the door behind her, then at nothing at all. “Nobody came in at nine. Nobody’s been in that room since Tuesday.”

It was a good lie. Vega had heard worse from people who were paid to tell them. But there was a wet ring on the bar by the back-room door, and a second glass upside down on the drainer, still beaded.

“Then hear this.” Lena leaned close enough that Vega could smell the lemon on her hands. “Go home. Don’t come back to the Blue Lamp tomorrow. And don’t ask Kit what he saw.”

Outside, the rain had turned to the fine kind that does not fall so much as hang. Vega walked the long way around, past the station, because it was the way Kit always walked.

At the corner of Harbor Street she stopped under the awning of the pawnshop and counted the windows above the Blue Lamp. Three dark, one lit. She stood there long enough for the light to go out.

In the morning the awning was rolled up and the pawnshop was shut, with a card in the window that said back at ten in a hand she knew from somewhere.
