“Then hear this.” Lena leaned close enough that Vega could smell the lemon on her hands. “Go home. Don’t come back tomorrow. And don’t ask Kit what he saw.”

* * *

Outside, the rain had turned to the fine kind that does not fall so much as hang. Vega walked the long way round, past the station, because it was the way Kit always walked.

She had asked him once, on the station steps, whether he would ever lie to her.

Kit said no.

At the corner of Harbour Street she stopped under the awning of the pawnshop and counted the windows above the Blue Lamp. Three dark, one lit. The lit one was the back room.

She did not go back in. She stood there long enough for the light to go out, and then a little longer, in case it came on again.
