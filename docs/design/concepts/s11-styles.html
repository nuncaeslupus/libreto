The pawnshop window held the usual: two clocks, a trumpet without its mouthpiece, a tray of rings nobody would ever come back for.

And, propped against the glass, a paperback she had not seen since she was fifteen: Rain over Harbor Street, its cover faded to the color of weak tea.

She had read it four times that summer. Her mother had taken it away the fifth time, saying that a girl who knew every street in a book would never learn the ones outside the door.

Vega went in. The bell over the door was the same flat note as the one at the Blue Lamp, and for a moment she was sure she had been here before.

“The book in the window,” she said. “How much?”
