Most of the bars on Harbor Street kept the same hours and the same furniture: a long counter, a brass rail, a jukebox that nobody fed after ten. The back room was for cards, or for whoever the owner owed a favor.

Hours and the law

The city licensed bars until two. Most bars closed at two; the bottles were locked at half past one. A bartender who kept pouring after that was the one who lost the license, not the owner.

“Nobody closed on time, and nobody was ever open late.” — a retired bartender, interviewed 1978

Lemon was the smell of a bar that had been cleaned; the owner’s bar smelled of beer. Lena would have carried the lemon home on her hands.
