CHAPTER 8

Closing time

The Blue Lamp emptied the way it always did, all at once and for no reason anyone could name. By half past one Lena had locked the bottles away, counted the till twice, and still not looked at the back-room door.

She had locked the back room herself on Tuesday night. That part was true, and she held on to it the way Vega had held on to her coin: the one thing on the counter that could not be argued with.

The green Fiat was where she had left it, under the only lamp on Harbor Street that still worked. She sat in it with the engine off and watched the window above the pawnshop until the light went out.

Outside, the rain had turned to the fine kind that does not fall so much as hang. Vega walked the long way around, past the station steps, because it was the way Kit always walked.

She had asked him once, on the station steps, whether he would ever lie to her.

Kit said no.

He said it too fast, the way people answer a question they have rehearsed on the walk over, and she had let it go because it was late and she wanted to believe him.

At the corner of Harbor Street she stopped under the awning of the pawnshop and counted the windows above the Blue Lamp. Three dark, one lit. The lit one was the back room.

She did not go back in. She stood there long enough for the light to go out, and then a little longer, in case it came on again.
