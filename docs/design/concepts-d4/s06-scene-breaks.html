“Lena,” she said quietly, “if he’s in trouble, I’d rather hear it from you.”

“Then hear this.” Lena leaned close enough that Vega could smell the lemon on her hands. “Go home. Don’t come back to the Blue Lamp tomorrow. And don’t ask Kit what he saw.”

Vega left the coin where it was and her glass untouched. She did not look at the back-room door on the way out, which took more effort than the walk home would.

At the corner of Harbor Street she stopped under the awning of the pawnshop and counted the windows above the Blue Lamp. Three dark, one lit. She stood there long enough for the light to go out.

Outside, the rain had started again by the time Vega woke, and the light through the blind was the flat gray of nine in the morning. She lay still and counted what she knew, which did not take long.
